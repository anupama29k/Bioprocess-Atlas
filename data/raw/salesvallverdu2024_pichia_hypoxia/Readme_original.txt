2. Methods for processing the data: 
The method for estimating discrete rates or time-dependent specific growth rate (µ(t)), specific substrate consumption rate (qS(t)), specific product (or by-product) formation rate (qP(t)), specific oxygen consumption rate (qO2(t)), and specific carbon dioxide production rate (qCO2(t)) was adapted from a study by Cos (2005). Initially, global state variables such as biomass (XV), substrate (SV), and product (PV) concentrations were estimated using off-line data collected during the feeding phase. The Curvefit Toolbox of Matlab R2009a was employed to smooth the curves of these global state variables, and their first time-derivatives were calculated.
On-line data on O2 and CO2 concentrations were utilized to calculate the oxygen uptake rate (OUR) and carbon dioxide evolution rate (CER). 
The specific rates of µ(t), qS(t), and qP(t) were then derived by applying their corresponding mass balances, whereas qO2(t) and qCO2(t) were determined by dividing the calculated OUR and CER by the biomass concentration measured from off-line samples. The uncertainties in these discrete specific rates were quantified through error propagation. As mentioned, the equations for these calculations can be found in the literature, specifically in the work of Cos (2005). 

- Cos, O. et al. (2005) «Combined effect of the methanol utilization (Mut) phenotype and gene dosage on recombinant protein production in Pichia pastoris fed-batch cultures.», Journal of biotechnology, 116(4), pp. 321-35. doi: 10.1016/j.jbiotec.2004.12.010.

For this study, a linear regression method was used to estimate the average specific rates, employing off-line data for some global state variables (i.e., biomass, substrate, and product concentrations) and on-line data for others (i.e., feed flow rate and O2 and CO2 concentrations). Yields are estimated as quotients between ratios (ri) or specific ratios (qi). 
For a more comprehensive description of the processing methods, please refer to the detailed information provided in the references below:

- Barrigón, J. M., Montesinos, J. L., & Valero, F. (2013). Searching the best operational strategies for Rhizopus oryzae lipase production in Pichia pastoris Mut+ phenotype: Methanol limited or methanol non-limited fed-batch cultures? Biochemical Engineering Journal, 75, 47–54. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bej.2013.03.018

- Garcia-Ortega, X. et al. (2013) «Fed-batch operational strategies for recombinant Fab production with Pichia pastoris using the constitutive GAP promoter», Biochemical Engineering Journal. Elsevier B.V., 79, pp. 172-181. doi: 10.1016/j.bej.2013.07.013.

4. Instruments, calibration and standards information:
For the lab-scale cultivations performed in a Biostat B fermenter (Sartorius Stedim, Goettingen, Germany), they had an operational volume of 5 L, with  initial batch volume was 2 L. Additionally, the chiller used for these fermentations was a Frigomix FX-1000 (Sartorius Stedim, Goettingen, Germany). On-line measuring probes were calibrated before each fermentation under operating conditions.

Glucose, as well as the potential fermentation by-products (ethanol, arabitol, and succinate) were detected via HPLC. An Ultimate 3000 system (Dionex, Sunnyvale, CA, USA) equipped with an ICSep Coregel 87H3 ion exchange column (Transgenomic, Omaha, NE, USA) was used for the analyses. The detection of each component was done with a 2410 Refractive Index Detector from Waters Corporation (Milford, MA, USA).Standards with precisely known concentrations were employed to calibrate the analytical method.

A BlueInOne FERM gas analyzer (BlueSens, Herten, Germany) was employed for real-time monitoring of CO2 and O2 molar fractions as well as absolute humidity in both chemostat and fed-batch cultures. These parameters were continuously recorded from the exhaust gas and intermittently measured from the inlet gas. To ensure precision in measurement, the gas analyzer underwent recalibration before each fed-batch experiment. The recalibration involved passing various gas mixtures with known CO2 and O2 concentrations through the analyzer, establishing a linear correlation between CO2/O2 concentrations and the sensor's electronic signal in millivolts.
The obtained data were utilized to compute key respirometric parameters, including the oxygen uptake rate (OUR), carbon dioxide evolution rate (CER), their corresponding specific rates (qO2 and qCO2), and the respiratory quotient (RQ). This comprehensive analysis provided insights into the metabolic activity of the cultures and their respiratory behavior throughout the experimental processes.


5. Environmental or experimental conditions:
All experiments were conducted at a controlled temperature of 25ºC and a pH of 5.


6. Quality-assurance procedures performed on the data:
In the various analytical techniques, replicates were performed, ensuring that the Relative Standard Deviation (RSD) is below a threshold considered as reliable. This value varies slightly depending on the analysis, always below 5%. For the calculated variables, the standard deviation was determined through error propagation.
